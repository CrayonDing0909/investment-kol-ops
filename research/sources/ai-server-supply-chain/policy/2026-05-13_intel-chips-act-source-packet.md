## Raw Facts

- US Department of Commerce awarded Intel up to $7.86 billion in direct
  funding under the CHIPS and Science Act (announced 2024-11-26).
- The funding supports Intel's commercial semiconductor manufacturing and
  advanced packaging projects in **Arizona, New Mexico, Ohio, and Oregon**.
- This is in addition to a separate $3 billion contract awarded for the
  **Secure Enclave program** (trusted manufacturing of leading-edge
  semiconductors for the US government).
- The award, combined with a 25% federal Investment Tax Credit, supports
  Intel's plans to invest more than **$100 billion** in the US over the next
  several years.
- The investment supports more than 10,000 Intel jobs, ~20,000 construction
  jobs, and 50,000+ indirect jobs.
- $65 million is set aside within the award for semiconductor workforce
  development: $56M for student / faculty training, $5M for childcare
  availability, $4M for CHIPS Women in Construction Framework.
- Intel currently employs ~45,000 people in the US.
- The CSIS report frames Intel as "the only US-headquartered firm within
  striking distance of regaining US capabilities at advanced process nodes",
  with TSMC and Samsung being the other two leading-edge firms but
  headquartered outside the US.
- TSMC fabricates roughly 90% of the world's most advanced chips (CSIS).
- Onshore US chipmaking accounts for ~10% of global capacity (CSIS).
- Intel reports a dozen customers using its 18A tool kit as of late 2024
  (CSIS).
- CSIS notes Intel's Amazon Web Services deal: custom Intel Xeon 6 chip on
  Intel 3 + new AI fabric chip on Intel 18A (multi-billion, multi-year,
  announced 2024).
- The Secure Enclave program builds on Intel's relationship with DOD via the
  RAMP-C (Rapid Assured Microelectronics Prototypes - Commercial) and SHIP
  (State-of-the-Art Heterogeneous Integrated Packaging) programs.
- Intel reported milestones on the industry's first commercial High-NA EUV
  lithography scanner with installation at Hillsboro, Oregon R&D site.
- FinancialContent (2026 commentary) states that the US has "effectively
  decoupled a significant portion of the AI supply chain from geopolitical
  vulnerabilities through domestic production of advanced semiconductors at
  the sub-2nm level"; Intel 18A is in high-volume manufacturing at Fab 52 in
  Ocotillo, Arizona as of early 2026.
- CSIS observes Intel does not expect new fabs to generate "meaningful"
  revenue until 2027.
- Intel Foundry 2023 revenue was $18.9B with an operating loss of ~$7B
  (CSIS).

## Source Reliability Notes

- Intel Newsroom CHIPS Act press release: **primary** for funding totals,
  geography of investment, and Intel's stated $100B investment commitment.
  But it is a corporate press release; investment "plans" are conditional.
- The press release contains a substantial Safe Harbor section listing many
  risks: foundry profitability, fab yield, geopolitical tensions, supply
  chain, ability to attract customers. Read these as the same forward-looking
  risk language as standard SEC disclosure.
- CSIS report: **high-quality-secondary**. Excellent for context, history,
  and policy framing. Includes some advocacy framing ("vital that the
  company remains viable") that should not be treated as fact.
- FinancialContent piece: **commentary / opinion**. The claim "US has
  effectively decoupled" should be treated as overstated framing.
- Note Pat Gelsinger is the Intel CEO **at the time of the 2024 press
  release**; Lip-Bu Tan is the current CEO (Q1 2026 source). CEO turnover
  may affect execution of investment plans.
